# Prologue — "The Thin Ice"  *(v5 — ship candidate: final swaps + fact-check passed)*

> **Mythos was the stories. Psyche is the mind that could not stop making them — and living in them.**
> *(Bridge line, under the title / on the opening card.)*

*Locked metaphor, one meaning the whole way — **walk, don't dwell:*** *the myth is the frozen lake
(bright, load-bearing for a while, fatal if you homestead) · crossing it = using the story · building
a house on it = lodging (image / wound / provisional life) · **solid ground on the far bank = Psyche.***
*The reader comes off Mythos already standing on the ice; Psyche is the shore. Ice is implied, never
quoted from the song.*

*Title: **The Thin Ice.** Slide-3 header stays **Solid Ground** (the far bank).*

---

## SLIDE 1 — The Mirror

You just walked through a world of gods and champions, and somewhere in it your chest tightened — a
betrayed son, a doomed hero, a throne worth weeping over. Stop on that feeling. Why should a quarrel
between kings who never lived reach into a stranger, in a century they could not have imagined, and
**pull?**

Because it was never a report from somewhere else. **It was a mirror, and the face in it was yours.**
A frozen lake is a mirror too. It hands you back your own face, and it will hold your weight — **as
long as you keep moving and never ask how deep the water is.**

To understand a myth, you have to turn from the story to the thing that makes it: **the mind.**
Twenty-five centuries ago Xenophanes caught that mind in the act. The Ethiopians make their gods dark
and flat-nosed; the Thracians make theirs pale and red-haired; and if horses could draw, their gods
would have hooves. We build makers and carve them in our own likeness — and not only our faces. Homer
and Hesiod, he said, hung on their gods everything shameful in men: **theft, adultery, deceit.** The
mind does not only paint our features on the heavens. It paints our **hungers** there, at a size we
can kneel to.

This is not a flaw to be ashamed of. It is the same machine that reads a cause behind the accident
and a **who** behind the silence — because a world run by persons is one a mind can hold. It will make
myths whether you allow it or not. So the question is never *whether* you mythologise. It is the one
that quietly decides a life: **do you cross the myth, or do you build on it?**

The gods were never out there, then. They came from the mind behind your forehead — the mind that
painted this bright surface and then mistook it for a floor. You have been walking on it your whole
life, and it has been handing you your own face. **Walking is allowed. Building is not.**

---

## SLIDE 2 — The Two Rooms

Your Mythos already showed you what it costs to stop crossing and move in. Twice — two houses built on
the same thin ice.

**One house is the image.** A champion grew so fused with his own legend that when a young warrior
faced him and asked, plainly, *are you the man the songs are about?* — he would not say yes. The boy
was his son; neither had ever seen the other's face. He had lived on that name for a lifetime, and the
ice held — **until the day it had to carry the weight of his own child. Then it gave way.** He learned
the truth from a token on the body, after the blade. The legend ate the boy.

**The other house is the wound.** A king grew two serpents from his shoulders. Cut them off and they
returned; the only thing that quieted them was to feed them the brains of the young, day after day. He
did not decide to become a monster — **he agreed to a feeding schedule.** He had mistaken a frozen
wound for solid land and raised a palace on it, and for a thousand years the young were fed to the
serpents to keep it from going under. *A wound allowed to write the rules will put the young on the
menu.*

Two houses, one lake. One man will not say his name; the other will not finish his meal; both mistook
a bright surface for solid ground. **The image will not say its name. The wound will not finish its
meal. Between them there is no tomorrow — only the crack, and then the cold.**

You will not live those plots. But you already know the ice. The shining life that has *not started
yet;* the old hurt that still sets the day; the grey that falls over an ordinary Tuesday because it is
not *it.* That grey is not wisdom. It is rent on a room you do not own — **the first faint creak under
a floor you were never meant to stand still on.**

---

## SLIDE 3 — Solid Ground

**A myth is not land. It is a crossing — and you carry something to the far side.**

The oldest tellers knew what that something was. The fairy tale does not invent the child's dragon;
the child has known the dragon since he first had an imagination. **What the tale hands him is a
figure who can kill it** — and then it expects him to walk back into his own day and swing. Your own
Mythos ends its serpent-king not with a spell but with a **blacksmith** — a man on solid ground, who
tears up the decree that calls the horror just, ties his leather apron to a spear, and lifts it. That is a myth used correctly: not a house he moved into, but a nerve he
carried to the far bank. The story was never a place to live. It was **a loan of nerve — spend it on
the crossing.** You have been admiring the dragon instead of fighting it.

That is where this series begins, and it is not another pantheon. It is the machine that built the
last one — the mind that paints a *who* on silence, a *destiny* on a delay, a *dragon* on a
difficulty. From here we stop kneeling to the shapes we painted on the heavens. We learn to hold the
myth as what it always was: a tool, not a god, and **never a house on the ice.**

**This series is the solid ground on the far side. From there, we take the machine apart.**
